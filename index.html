Research Assistant · University of Waterloo

Hi, I’m Jiarong Liang. I’m currently a Research Assistant at the University of Waterloo, advised by Wenhu Chen, working on natural language processing (NLP) and large language models (LLMs).

I completed my B.S. in Computer Science at South China University of Technology and my M.S. at Washington University in St. Louis.

✨ I am actively looking for PhD opportunities for 2026/2027, research collaborations, and internships in LLM/VLM-related areas. If you have interesting ideas or are interested in my research, feel free to reach out anytime.

News

All news →
  • Oct 20, 2026

    Released StopLoss, a small monitor that learns from finished runs to predict when a coding agent is about to fail, so doomed runs can be stopped early. Read the paper, browse the project page, or grab the models and training data.

  • Jul 14, 2026

    New preprint: FIM-Midtraining mid-trains code LLMs to fill in masked functions, a task that mirrors how an agent calls a tool and uses the result, and lifts SWE-Bench-Verified by about 3 points. The paper is on arXiv, and the checkpoints and 2.6B-token corpus are on Hugging Face.

  • Apr 07, 2026

    SWE-Next is out! It turns real merged GitHub pull requests into 2,308 self-verifying tasks for training software-engineering agents. See the paper, the project page, and the dataset.

  • Feb 08, 2026

    Introducing VisPhyWorld, which tests whether multimodal LLMs really understand physics by asking them to rebuild a video as runnable simulator code. The paper and benchmark data are available now.

  • Oct 01, 2025

    Our VisCoder2 models (3B to 32B) write, run, and self-debug visualization code in 12 programming languages. Details are in the paper; the models, the 679K-sample training set, and the VisPlotBench benchmark live in our Hugging Face collection.
